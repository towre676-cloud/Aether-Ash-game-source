Paint an original production material atlas for the game Ash & Aether. Exactly FOUR water-surface swatches in a precise TWO BY TWO grid, edge-to-edge square cells, no gutters or lines, on a square canvas. Each cell is a flat orthographic view looking straight DOWN at a broad quiet natural water surface. All four are seamless-looking continuous materials without a horizon, shoreline, shore rocks, reeds, floating objects, fish, plants, lily pads, foam crests, specular sun disc, bubbles, text or labels. Opaque RGB texture; no transparency. The entire image is only the four textures.

Style is subtle hand-painted gouache and watercolor material, restrained elegant storybook fantasy realism, NOT a photo and NOT shiny 3D game water. Large relaxed washes and gentle current-like brush patterns, tiny barely visible ripples, low contrast and very low spatial frequency. Absolutely NO small tiled bright caustic lattice, no white crosshatch, no repeating diamonds, no sharp cell-like pattern. Very dark subdued underwater hints can imply depth but no identifiable large object. A travelling character and environment props will sit above this texture; the water must support them quietly. Think shaded water between riverbanks in a crafted RPG environment, not a decorative wallpaper pattern.

Top RIGHT: MANGROVE TIDAL WATER — muted olive teal and dark bottle green, shaded muddy depths and subtle translucent ochre silt wisps, very quiet broad value shifts.
Bottom LEFT: CORAL SHOAL WATER — restrained luminous blue turquoise and softened pale sea-green, broad transparent shallows, slight cooler deep pools, without bright caustic geometry.
Bottom RIGHT: SALTGLASS LAGOON — desaturated pale mineral sage and grey aqua, broad milky mineral clouds suspended in calm water, thin very soft ripples.

Each swatch keeps similar brightness at its left/right and top/bottom edges so alternate reflected repetitions join smoothly. No framing, no obvious material boundaries except the straight 2×2 divisions. High quality original painterly surfaces, four distinct regional color identities.
